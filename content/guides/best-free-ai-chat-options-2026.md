Every major AI chat app advertises a `$0` plan in late 2026, yet "free" means five very different things depending on which company you pick. One gives you unlimited plain text, another gives you code-running Artifacts with strict 5-hour caps, and another bundles Deep Research but gates Gmail integration behind a paid tier.

## Which Free Tool Should You Use for Each Job?

1. **Daily brainstorming, quick Q&A, and text drafting:** Start with **ChatGPT Free** because everyday text chats have no hard 5-hour lockout.
2. **Structured documents, interactive web previews (Artifacts), and clean writing:** Use **Claude Free**, saving your allowance for longer editing or coding sessions where Artifacts shine.
3. **Source-cited fact lookups:** Use **Perplexity Free** when you want clickable footnotes instead of unverified memory.
4. **Google ecosystem research & Canvas drafts:** Use **Gemini Free**, and consider **Google AI Plus ($4.99/mo)** only if you specifically want Gemini embedded inside Gmail and Docs.
5. **Fast summaries & social/news context:** Use **Grok Free** within its weekly allowance, or pay $8/mo (Plus) for higher limits.

---

## When Should You Actually Pay?

Do not subscribe to three $20/month plans at once. Stay on `$0` until you hit a recurring wall in **one** workflow:
- If you hit **file/image/reasoning limits** every workday in ChatGPT -> test **Go ($8/mo)** or **Plus ($20/mo)**.
- If you constantly hit **5-hour caps** in Claude -> **Pro ($17/mo annual or $20/mo monthly)** adds 5x+ usage plus Opus and scheduled tasks.
- If you want **AI inside Gmail/Docs** -> **Google AI Plus ($4.99/mo)** is currently the lowest-priced entry point.
- If you run **automated scripts or batch tasks** -> skip consumer subscriptions and use a pay-as-you-go API key (`gpt-5-nano`, `Claude Haiku 5.5`, or `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`), which often costs under $2/month for light solo volume.
